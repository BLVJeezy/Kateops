# Optios leaves solo stylists paying for teams

Optios is a broad, Belgium-built, browser-based salon suite (agenda, online reserveren, klantenfiche, kassa, producten, HR, rapporten, marketing, plus newer add-ons such as Optios Pay, AI voice and chat assistants, managed ads and a white-label client app). It is built for team salons, not one-person ones. **It publishes no euro prices at all.** Its smallest plan, Business, covers "up to 4 users". Features that matter most to a solo hairstylist are locked into higher tiers: stock management needs Business Plus, and online deposits (voorschotten), gift vouchers, klantenkaarten and subscriptions need Pro ([Optios prijzen](https://www.optios.net/prijzen)). Public sentiment is thin: Capterra has 8 reviews averaging 3.4/5. The complaints are commercial rather than about the interface: misleading sales promises, refused cancellations, disputed notice periods and unexpected debits ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/166897/Optios/reviews/)). The transparent competitors are much cheaper for a solo: Salonized starts at €21/month excl. VAT and Fresha at about $19.95/month. Marketplace players (Treatwell, Fresha, Booksy Boost) make their money from 20–35% new-client commissions. That leaves a clear opening for Kitoko by Kate: a phone-first, flat-priced, commission-free tool that bundles deposits, vouchers, beurtenkaarten and reminders, and puts colour formulas on every appointment. The biggest caveat is that no public source documents specific Optios UI pain points such as a cramped phone agenda. Those need hands-on confirmation through the 14-day free trial before the design leans on them.

## Twelve modules, but a solo needs about five

Optios's own navigation lists Meer klanten, Minder no-shows, AI tooling, Online reserveren, Digitale agenda, Kassasysteem, Producten beheren, Klantenbeheer, HR module, Voice AI, Salon marketing hub and Optios Pay ([optios.net](https://www.optios.net/)). Its help center has **219 articles across 23 collections**. Online booking (32 articles), Products (21), Customers (20) and Register (20) are the deepest modules, so they are a useful map of where the product's weight sits ([help.optios.net](https://help.optios.net/en/)). Mobile is the weakest part of the product. Staff use the responsive web app, which is the same side-by-side staff-column calendar "optimized for smaller screens", in day and week views only ([Optios agenda](https://en.optios.net/digitale)). The native iOS app is iPhone-only and English-only. Its releases from 1.0 (April 2023) to **5.0 (27 February 2024)** all carry the release note "User Interface Improved", and it has had no update since ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/nl/app/optios/id6447364622?l=en-GB)). No Google Play staff app was found. Optios needs an internet connection and describes no offline mode. Several help titles name features but were not opened: "vind een vrij moment", the exact appointment-type labels (afspraak / last minute / workshop / blok), and whether beurtenkaarten exist as a named product. Treat those labels as likely, not verified.

## No published price, and the one-user cost is unknown

Optios sells by quote only. The price "hangt af van je pakket en het aantal gebruikers", there is a 14-day trial with no credit card, and the company promises no hidden fees ([Prijzen](https://www.optios.net/prijzen)). Capterra also shows only "Contact Vendor for Pricing" ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/166897/Optios/)). SMS reminders and campaigns cost extra per message at an unpublished rate. Email reminders are included ([FAQ](https://en.optios.net/faq)). The white-label app and Optios Pay are also quote-only or fee-based without published rates. The one number Optios does give is general: it says salon software in Belgium and the Netherlands typically costs "a few dozen to a few hundred euros per month" ([FAQ](https://en.optios.net/faq)). **What a solo stylist would actually pay for Business, or for Pro to get deposits, cannot be verified from public sources.** The research also did not find the contract length, the notice period in the general terms, any setup fee or the SMS rate. A trial signup or a quote request is the only way to fill that gap.

Competitors are much more transparent. Fresha's dollar figures and the Treatwell subscription come from competitor blogs and are indicative.

Two details matter in Belgium. First, Salonized has added **21% Belgian VAT** to its invoices since September 2023. For a stylist under the vrijstellingsregeling (small-business VAT exemption), that VAT is a real extra cost ([Salonized help](https://help.salonized.com/en/articles/8167117-vat-application-in-belgium)). Second, Treatwell has owned Salonized since October 2024, and Pabau warns that packages and pricing may change as the two merge ([Pabau](https://pabau.com/nl/blog/salonized-prijzen/)). Commission is the hidden cost of the marketplace model. Pabau's example: a salon with €3,000 of monthly marketplace revenue and 60% new clients pays about **€479/month** to Treatwell ([Pabau](https://pabau.com/fr/blog/treatwell-tarif/)). Fresha can still charge commission when a client it brought in later books through the salon's own site ([GlossGenius](https://glossgenius.com/blog/fresha-pricing)). For a stylist with a loyal local client base, marketplace reach is worth little and costs a lot.

## Complaints target contracts, not screens

Optios's evidence base is small, and its positive and negative reviews come from different periods. Capterra's 8 reviews split into 4 five-star, 1 four-star and 3 one-star, with nothing in between. The scores are ease of use 3.5 and customer service 3.4 ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/166897/Optios/reviews/)), and value for money 3.3 ([Capterra FR](https://www.capterra.fr/software/166897/optios)). The five-star reviews cluster within a few days in August 2023. The one-star reviews are from November 2024 to May 2025 and describe "no service and no personal contact" and a salesperson who gave false information. One reviewer said promised features could not be set up, they paid over €1,000, and cancellation was refused after the salesperson left. Another alleged unauthorized debits ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/166897/Optios/reviews/)). A 2023 Test-Aankoop complaint titled "Pesterijen" says that after the salon moved to Salonkee, Optios kept putting its old booking page back online, which caused double bookings. The salon and Optios also disagreed on the notice period: one month according to the salon, three according to Optios ([Test-Aankoop](https://www.test-aankoop.be/klagen/publieke-klachten/pesterijen/CPTBE01774004-68)). Optios claims 4.8/5 on Google, but those reviews could not be checked.

The praise shows what any replacement must match. Reviewers credit Optios with fewer no-shows, easy online booking, reminders, client notes and growth reports ([Capterra FR](https://www.capterra.fr/software/166897/optios)). A Ghent salon owner calls last-minute offers her favourite feature because they refill cancelled 2–3 hour colouring slots ([Optios testimonials](https://en.optios.net/testimonials)). The only small-salon voice, a beginner owner, liked the ease of use but said the price "can seem high for small salons with limited budgets" ([Capterra FR](https://www.capterra.fr/software/166897/optios)). One reviewer waited two weeks for a demo, and onboarding relies on e-learning and an Optios expert ([FAQ](https://en.optios.net/faq)). Both suggest the product is not self-explanatory.

**No public review mentions a cluttered phone agenda, truncated text or too many checkout steps.** Side-by-side staff columns squeezed into a phone browser are a likely source of exactly those problems. That remains a hypothesis until someone tests it, either in the trial or by talking to Optios users locally.

## What the competitors teach about solo-first design

Fresha sets the bar for user experience. A competitor comparison rates it 4.8 for ease of use, with a "clean, intuitive mobile-first interface" and onboarding live in under 30 minutes. The same source rates Booksy 4.4, Planity 4.2 and Treatwell 4.1 ([Altegio](https://alteg.io/en/blog/13-best-online-booking-software-for-beauty-businesses-in-europe-in-2026/)). Those ratings come from a competitor, so read them as directional. The marketplace apps share one structural weakness: clients build loyalty to the platform's app rather than to the stylist's brand, and there is no white-label booking ([Altegio](https://alteg.io/en/blog/13-best-online-booking-software-for-beauty-businesses-in-europe-in-2026/)). Each competitor also has one pattern worth borrowing. Optios ties checkout to the appointment and deducts stock automatically ([Optios](https://en.optios.net/kassasysteem)). Salonized puts first-visit discount codes and last-minute discounts directly in the booking widget ([Pabau](https://pabau.com/nl/blog/salonized-prijzen/)). Planity's terminal suggests tips and emails receipts ([Pabau](https://pabau.com/fr/blog/planity-pro-prix/)). Hair-salon CRM guidance treats formula notes on the appointment screen and booking the next visit at checkout as buying criteria ([SchedulingKit](https://www.schedulingkit.com/crm/hair-salons)). None of the competitor vendor pages confirmed colour-formula fields or before/after photos. Optios is the only one where kleuringen are documented. A solo tool that makes formulas and photos central would be ahead of most of the field.

Fiscal compliance does not block a lean build. Belgium's geregistreerd kassasysteem (GKS / witte kassa) obligation covers places where meals are consumed and caterers above €25,000 turnover. Hairdressers are not named ([Vlaanderen.be](https://www.vlaanderen.be/geregistreerde-kassa-witte-kassa)). As of February 2026, extension to other sectors was still undecided ([VATupdate](https://www.vatupdate.com/2026/02/04/belgium-extends-gks-2-0-rollout-for-hospitality-retail-inclusion-decision-still-pending/)). The government has, however, signalled plans for other "fraud-sensitive industries" ([Fiscal-requirements.com](https://www.fiscal-requirements.com/news/3680-is-the-usage-of-certified-cash-register-systems-expanding-to-other-sectors-in-belgium)). The product therefore needs correct BTW on kasticket and factuur, VAT handling for deposits, an accountant export and Peppol for B2B invoices, which matches what Optios offers. Its transaction data should be structured so a certified FDM/GKS module can be added later. Nothing after February 2026 was found, so check FPS Finance before launch.

| Design pillar for Kitoko by Kate | Answers which gap |
|---|---|
| One flat public price (roughly €20–30/month), monthly cancellable, self-serve data export, no demo needed | Optios's quote-only pricing, contract and notice disputes, offboarding problems |
| Voorschotten, cadeaubonnen, beurtenkaarten and light stock in the single plan | Optios gates these behind Pro and Business Plus |
| WhatsApp/SMS reminders included | Optios and Salonized charge per SMS; Fresha bundles them |
| A single-column, phone-first day timeline (dagweergave) with offline tolerance | Optios's desktop-style staff columns and online-only web app |
| Kleuringen and photo history shown on the appointment, plus a "volgende afspraak" prompt at checkout | Rarely documented by competitors |
| A stylist-branded booking page and PWA, with no marketplace commission | Treatwell/Fresha/Booksy commissions and client lock-in to the platform |
| Last-minute gap filling and a wachtlijst | Optios's most-loved feature, which must be matched |

## Conclusion

Optios is not weak on features. For a one-person salon its problems are packaging and trust. It sells a team-salon suite through quotes and sales calls, then locks the solo stylist's most important tools (deposits, vouchers, loyalty) in its most expensive tier. So the opportunity for Kitoko by Kate is less about adding features than removing friction: transparent pricing, no lock-in, a phone screen designed for one person's day, and the colour history that makes a hairdresser's clients hers. The commercial complaints against Optios are the clearest product spec available. An alternative that lets a stylist leave cleanly with her data earns trust that Optios's reviews show it has lost.

The main open risks are factual and cheap to close. Optios's real one-user price, its contract terms, and whether its phone agenda actually frustrates solo users are all unverified. A 14-day trial account and two or three conversations with local Optios users would turn the design hypotheses into confirmed requirements. Checking Fresha's Belgian euro pricing and the GKS sector-extension decision would keep the positioning honest at launch.
